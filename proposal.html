You're selling online with no policies, and you're doing trauma work without a scope note

Three settings that should concern you:

  • No privacy policy, no terms of service, no return policy are configured for your store, while you're accepting payments and collecting names and email addresses.
  • No cookie or privacy notice is enabled on the site.
  • No scope-of-practice statement anywhere explaining that coaching is not therapy or counselling, plus crisis resources for a woman in immediate danger.
Why it matters, and I say this plainly: In Canada, collecting personal information and emailing a mailing list carry legal expectations (PIPEDA and CASL), and a store without published policies weakens your position if there's ever a dispute. More importantly: you serve women leaving abuse. A clear "coaching is not a substitute for therapy or emergency help" statement, alongside a crisis line, is an act of care, and it makes you look more professional, not less.

One more thing worth a quiet check

Your homepage displays the post-nominals CSW, CPC, BCC. Credentials are a sincere strength of yours, but in Ontario some professional titles are protected by law, and coaching itself is not regulated. Before any redesign, it's worth 20 minutes confirming that each designation you display is one you're entitled to advertise, and that the way it's written matches the certifying body's guidance. I'd rather raise it now, quietly, than have someone else raise it later in a way that costs you. Consider it a footnote, not a criticism. This is exactly the kind of detail a good web partner should be watching for.

Brand direction: beauty from ashes, made visual

Four decisions carry the whole feel.

Palette

Deep plum and warm ivory as the foundation: intimate, dignified, not clinical. Sage green for calm and growth. A restrained gold for scriptural accents. Soft blush for warmth. Nothing neon, nothing corporate, nothing "church clip-art."

Typography

One elegant serif for headings, it reads like a letter from someone who cares, paired with one clean, highly legible sans for body copy, because your reader is often tired and on a phone. Large type, generous spacing, short paragraphs.

Imagery

Real women, real rooms, real light: your Saturday gatherings, your book, you. Warm and unposed rather than stock. This is the fastest way to look like the safe place you actually are.

Voice

Keep yours. Second person, short sentences, no jargon, no "unlock your potential." You already write like a human; that's a competitive advantage most coaches pay copywriters to fake.

Signature line

"You survived what happened to you. Now it's time to heal, rebuild and rediscover the woman God created you to be.", promoted from paragraph to hero headline.

Trust architecture

Credentials, the six-step journey, pricing with no surprises, a free 15-minute call, and testimonials beside every ask. Proof at the exact moment doubt appears.

Questions you might have

Will I lose my content or my Google history?

No. Everything that works stays, and every page you've ranked for is redirected properly so nothing is lost. Your blog posts, your book page and your pricing all survive; they simply get rehoused.

Do I have to learn something new?

No. We stay on Squarespace, which you already use. I'll record a short session showing you how to make everyday changes: a new event date, a new blog post, a new testimonial.

What if I'm not ready for the whole redesign?

Then do the Foundation Fix. In two weeks your site stops losing people, and you can decide about the rest when you've seen what changes.

What if I can't afford it right now?

Tell me. We can stage it, spread it over a few months, or start with only the free fixes and the booking page. I'd rather build a long relationship than lose you over timing.

Can I make changes myself afterwards?

Yes, and I'd want you to. I'll hand over cleanly: an editable site, a short guide, and support for thirty days after launch.

How do you handle the testimonials, given privacy?

Carefully. First name and city only, written permission for each one, no identifying detail, and any story that could expose someone is generalised or left out. Your clients' safety comes before marketing.
