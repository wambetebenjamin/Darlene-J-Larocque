## The five findings that sell this job

Lead with these. Each one is verifiable in under a minute, which is what makes them land.

1. **"Book and pay for a session" → a blank page.** Her coaching page prices sessions at $65 / $180 / $330. The button leads to `/bookings`, which displays only the word *"bOOKINGS"*. She has no way to take money and no scheduler.
2. **There is no Contact page.** `/contact` returns a Squarespace 404. Her only form is buried on the Bible-study page, her phone number is hidden from mobile entirely, and **two different Gmail addresses appear on the site**: `mtwlifecoach@` in a blog post and `mtwlifwcoach@` in the site settings. One of them is wrong.
3. **A yoga studio is still live on her site.** `/joinnow` advertises a Free plan, *$15/mo Unlimited Yoga & Meditation* and a *$3,500 200-hr Teacher Training*, leftover template demo content. There's also a blank `/about-1`, and `/about-2` holds her real biography at an address nobody would guess.
4. **Google has nothing to work with.** No site description, no meta descriptions, page titles reading "Store 2", "Blog 1" and "bOOKINGS", and no Barrie/keyword signals. Meanwhile other Christian counsellors in Barrie rank on page one, with thinner credentials than hers.
5. **Zero proof.** No testimonial, review, event photo or rating on the entire site, for a $65 to $330 ask with a client group that has every reason to be cautious. Plus no speaker page, despite being available internationally.

**Two more that show you went deeper than a skim:**

- **The credential check.** She displays `CSW, CPC, BCC`. In Ontario some professional titles are protected. Raise it gently as something *you'd* verify for her, never as an accusation. (This is in the proposal as a soft footnote, phrased carefully. Keep that framing.)
- **Legal housekeeping.** Her store has no privacy policy, no terms, no return policy and no cookie notice, while taking payments and collecting personal data in Canada (PIPEDA/CASL). She also has no coaching-vs-therapy scope statement, which matters a great deal for a trauma-focused practice.

## Talking points if she replies

- **"I paid someone to build that."** Understood, and the design isn't the problem. It's that it was never finished: three pages left in, the booking system never connected, and nobody checking the details afterwards. That's what I do.
- **"I don't have the budget right now."** Then start with the free five fixes, and we'll talk about the booking page because that one directly makes you money.
- **"Can't I just fix it myself in Squarespace?"** Absolutely, and I'd encourage it. The appendix in the proposal is a prioritised checklist you can work through on your own. I'm offering to do the parts that are slow, technical or need writing.
- **"I don't want to lose my Google ranking."** Nothing that ranks gets deleted. Everything is redirected properly. We stay on Squarespace, so you keep the platform you already know.
- **"I'm not sure about putting testimonials up."** Say it first: first name and city only, written permission for each one, nothing identifying, and any story that could expose someone gets generalised or left out. Client safety before marketing. That answer alone will set you apart.
- **"Who are you, exactly?"** Be ready with two or three relevant examples of your work and a link. The concept homepage is your strongest portfolio piece for this conversation; mention it early.

## What I verified, and what to double-check before quoting

Everything in the proposal's findings section is based on the live site: all twelve published pages, the sitemap, the site's own configuration and store settings, and the visitor paths a client would actually take. The blank booking page, the missing contact page, the yoga page, the two email addresses, the three empty shop categories, the empty `/about-1` and the page titles were all confirmed directly.

Two things to confirm before you present, so you're never caught out:

1. **The email addresses.** Two different addresses appear: one in a blog post, one in the site settings. I wrote it as "one of these is wrong" rather than guessing which. Ask her which is correct.
2. **Which page her biography *should* live on.** `/about` currently serves the homepage, `/about-1` is blank and `/about-2` holds the real bio. Confirm which URL she'd prefer to keep before promising redirects.

Also worth knowing: the site's own settings confirm no privacy policy, terms or return policy are configured, no cookie banner is enabled, product reviews are switched off, shipping defaults to the United States rather than Canada, and the mobile info bar that would show her email, phone, location and hours is disabled for all four fields, even though her business hours are saved in the system. That's a lot of easy, high-impact work sitting in the settings screens alone.

## A note on tone

The proposal is deliberately warm before it is critical: it opens with a personal note, dedicates a full section to what's already strong, and frames the findings as *unfinished* rather than *bad*. That's not softening; it's the reason the document will get read rather than resented. She has been doing this work for years and her site reflects her calling.

Keep that tone in every conversation. You're not rescuing her from a bad website. You're offering to finish something she started, so that the women who need her can actually find her.

**And the good news you should genuinely believe:** the hard parts are already done. She has a clear calling, rare credentials, a published book, a monthly live event and a voice that makes women feel seen. She doesn't need a new story; she needs a doorway that opens.
